01 / INDUSTRIAL APPLICATIONS

CALCULUS IS
ALREADY AT WORK.

Integration converts changing quantities into meaningful totals — the energy generated, fluid stored, distance travelled, and charge delivered.

PV

Renewable energy

A solar plant receives changing irradiance throughout the day. Engineers integrate power over time to determine total energy yield.

E = ∫₀ᵀ P(t) dt
P(t): instantaneous power [kW] · t: time [h] · E: energy [kWh]
Idealised clear-day yield: 917 kWh
Q̇

Chemical & process engineering

Process tanks receive time-varying inflow. Integration tracks the accumulated material and helps prevent overflow.

V(t) = V₀ + ∫₀ᵗ Q(τ) dτ
Q(t): volumetric flow [L/min] · V: volume [L] · τ: time [min]
Accumulated in 30 min: 1,050 L
v(t)

Robotics & automation

A controller measures velocity. Integrating the velocity profile gives displacement, which determines where the robot will be.

Δx = ∫₀ᵀ v(t) dt
v(t): velocity [m/s] · t: time [s] · Δx: displacement [m]
Displacement in 12 s: 30.0 m
Σ

Manufacturing

Production rate varies with machine speed, changeovers and downtime. The integral gives the total units produced over a shift.

N = ∫₀ᵀ r(t) dt
r(t): production rate [units/h] · T: operating time [h] · N: units
Output in an 8 h shift: 3,600 units
I(t)

Electrical engineering

Current changes as a circuit operates. Integrating current gives transferred charge; integrating power gives consumed energy.

q = ∫ I(t) dt · E = ∫ P(t) dt
I: current [A] · q: charge [C] · P: power [W] · E: energy [J]
Charge in 1 h: 28,800 C
∬

Civil & structural engineering

A non-uniform pressure or load acts over a surface. Double integration estimates the resultant force or total material quantity.

F = ∬ᴬ p(x,y) dA
p(x,y): pressure [kPa] · dA: area element [m²] · F: force [kN]
Force over 20 m²: 240 kN

04 / MATHEMATICAL VISUALISATION ENGINE

THE MATHEMATICS
BEHIND THE MOTION.

Integration is the continuous sum of infinitely many small contributions. In engineering, those contributions may represent power, flow, velocity, force or production rate.

CONCEPT / 01

Accumulation

If r(t) is a rate, its integral over time gives the total accumulated quantity. Units multiply: (units/s) × s = units.

A = ∫ₐᵇ r(t) dt
CONCEPT / 02

Definite integral

The definite integral returns a signed net accumulation over an interval. Area below the axis is counted negatively.

∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx = F(b) − F(a)
CONCEPT / 03

Indefinite integral

An antiderivative is a family of functions whose derivative equals the integrand. The arbitrary constant is essential.

∫ f(x) dx = F(x) + C
CONCEPT / 04

Fundamental theorem

Differentiation and integration are inverse operations under the theorem's continuity conditions.

d/dx ∫ₐˣ f(t)dt = f(x)
THE ENGINEERING IDEA

Rates describe what is happening now. Integrals tell you what has happened in total.

Quantity = ∫ Rate(t) dt

For example, power is the rate of energy transfer. Integrating power over time gives energy. Velocity is the rate of change of position. Integrating velocity gives displacement. Always check the physical units before interpreting the result.

07 / TECHNICAL REFERENCE

THE ENGINEERING
GLOSSARY.

A quick reference for the mathematical and physical terms used throughout the virtual laboratories.

Integrand

The function being integrated. In a rate model, the integrand is often the instantaneous rate of accumulation.

Antiderivative

A function F(x) whose derivative is f(x). The indefinite integral is F(x) + C.

Riemann sum

A finite sum of rectangle areas used to approximate a definite integral over a partition.

Trapezoidal rule

A numerical method that approximates the curve by straight-line segments and sums trapezoid areas.

Simpson's 1/3 rule

A numerical integration method using quadratic interpolating arcs over pairs of subintervals; it requires an even number of subintervals.

Accumulation

The total quantity formed by adding a continuously changing rate over an interval.

Displacement

The signed change in position, found by integrating velocity with respect to time.

Volumetric flow rate

Fluid volume passing a cross-section per unit time, commonly measured in m³/s or L/min.

Dimensional consistency

An equation is dimensionally consistent when each term has compatible physical units.

Using every laboratory

Choose a lab tab and change its sliders. Read each numerical output with its unit, then inspect the graph or equipment view. Most controls recalculate immediately. Use RESET to restore the starting conditions; use play or animate controls to run a sequence.

01 — Riemann sum and numerical integration

Choose x², sin(x), eˣ or a linear function. Set lower and upper limits, number of rectangles and left, right or midpoint sampling. The rectangles approximate signed area. Compare the approximation with the analytical integral. Use Animate Convergence to watch the partition count increase.

02 — Solar power generation

Peak output sets maximum power, daylight duration defines the time interval, cloud coverage reduces the idealised profile, and elapsed daylight controls the accumulated portion. Energy is the integral of power over time: kW × h = kWh.

03 — Fluid flow and storage tank

Set nominal flow, elapsed time, initial volume and capacity. The pump model includes a small sinusoidal ripple. Final volume equals initial volume plus accumulated inlet flow. The tank indicates when the result exceeds capacity.

04 — Robotics and motion

Set acceleration, initial velocity and duration. Velocity is integrated to obtain displacement. Negative acceleration can slow or reverse the robot; displacement is signed and is not always the same as total path length.

05 — Electrical energy and charge

Set mean current, supply voltage and operating time. Charge is the integral of current in amperes over seconds, giving coulombs. Energy is the integral of power over time; convert watts × seconds to joules, or kilowatts × hours to kWh.

06 — Sensor log and measured samples

Load the solar, flow or current preset log, edit any reading, add or remove samples, then read the accumulated total. Trapezoidal integration is used for uneven timestamps; Simpson's 1/3 rule applies automatically when the log has an odd count of evenly spaced samples. Blank or invalid rows are skipped, so a partial edit never corrupts the total.

Worked example

If a machine operates at a constant production rate of 450 units/hour for 8 hours, total production is ∫₀⁸ 450 dt = 450 × 8 = 3,600 units. Units/hour multiplied by hours gives units.

Common mistakes

  • Confusing power (kW) with energy (kWh).
  • Forgetting to convert hours to seconds when charge is required in coulombs.
  • Interpreting signed displacement as total distance when velocity changes sign.
  • Comparing quantities without checking units.
  • Assuming numerical methods are exact; they approximate the integral and have method-dependent error.

Model scope

The simulations are transparent educational models: idealised sinusoidal solar output, nominal flow with pump ripple, constant-acceleration motion and a DC load with current ripple. Real industrial systems require measured data, losses, operating constraints and validated engineering models.
